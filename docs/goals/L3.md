# L3 — Mass and materials leftovers

**Status:** ready at `e400beb` (the sha the gate ran on; the one commit after it changes this file only), gate log target/verify-L3.log — 14 gates, 585.65 s.
**Branch:** `fix/mass-and-materials`
**Wave:** 3 · **Kind:** Rust + web · **Depends on:** B0 (and G2 merged — both touch the worker) · **Migrations:** 0051

## Why

Goal 5 (materials and mass) recorded five things it did not build (ROADMAP, goal 5 "Recorded, not built"). Each is
small; together they make mass trustworthy on every part page.

## Facts checked (as of `3964524`)

1. Revisions recorded before bridge 8 have no centre of mass until re-ingested, and the comparison says so.
2. The material and tag facets' range is untested; only the format facet's is.
3. A part's own mass is not on its page — only its change, in the history and the comparison — so a hobby library,
   which keeps no revisions, shows no mass anywhere.
4. A part whose file names a material cannot be marked as holding none: the × on its only material hands it straight
   back, and the page says nothing.
5. A STEP material named in more than 64 characters: its density box cannot save.

## Rules

- Owns: `crates/lapidary-api/src/densities.rs`, the part page's mass section, `crates/lapidary-db/src/repo.rs` for
  these items only (wave 3: nothing else edits it then), migration `0051`, and a re-derive path in
  `crates/lapidary-ingest` for item 1 (after G2 merged).
- Mass is mesh-derived unless it came from B-rep: labelled approximate where it is, as everywhere.

## Stages

One stage per item, each with its test first: (1) a queued re-derive of the centre of mass for revisions without one,
and the comparison saying it is on its way; (2) range tests for the material and tag facets; (3) the part's own mass
on its page, with its source and "approximate" where it applies; (4) an explicit "holds no material" that survives the
file naming one (`0051`); (5) material names over 64 characters saved, or refused saying the limit — decide, record.
Mutation-check each rule.

## Record

`cargo xtask verify slice`: **14 gates green in 585.65s** (`test` 182.61s, `export-bindings` 58.19s,
`web tests` 32.58s), 9m46s wall including two waits behind another lane's compile lock. Log at
`target/verify-L3.log`.

All five items built, tested and mutation-checked. **Sixteen mutations, sixteen caught** — and three of
them caught work of mine that was wrong: two test legs that proved nothing, and a sweep that would have
re-queued a CAD revision at every worker start for ever (both below).

**1. A centre of mass for revisions that have none.** `PgRevisions::centreless_revisions(limit,
formats)` finds revisions of live parts of a file in `formats` whose `volume > 0` and whose
`mass_props_json` is NULL; `WorkerHandler::enqueue_centreless_revisions` queues a
`Derive{TessellationL0}` for each at worker start, beside the shape sweep, capped at 5,000 a start.
`derive_one`'s L0 branch now records the centre beside the shape profile, which is what makes the
queued job fill it in.

The goal's fact 1 said "revisions recorded before bridge 8", and the real population is not that.
A pre-8 STEP revision the **existing** stale-derivative sweep already re-reads, because
`self.bridge_version` is inside the kernel version it keys on and the `Structure` branch has always
recorded a centre. A **mesh** revision from before `measure::measured` computed centres it never
finds: `MeshKernel::version` is `"<format>-1+glb-3+cpu-1"`, which names the format, the GLB writer
and the rasteriser and nothing about how it measures, so those rungs are current and no rebuild is
ever queued. Meshes are the whole population this item exists for, and the L0 branch is the only
path that can reach one — a mesh has no `structure` rung at all.

**So `formats` is `MESH_EXTENSIONS`, and that is a correctness rule, not a narrowing.** A CAD
revision here would queue a job that fails `Permanent` on a worker built without a CAD kernel, and
`enqueue_if_absent` ignores failed rows, so that revision would fail at every worker start for ever
— the board's "permanently-failing file re-attempted by every scan", in a new place. On a worker
that *does* have a CAD kernel it would read the same STEP file twice, once per sweep. Both sweeps'
predicates are now disjoint by format. `volume > 0`, not `volume IS NOT NULL`, is the other half:
the mesh kernel gates its volume on `is_watertight` and its centre on
`is_watertight && signed_volume != 0.0`, so a closed mesh enclosing nothing has a volume of 0 and no
centre for ever.

The comparison says `centreQueued` — "Not recorded yet — a re-read is queued for it" — for the three
centre axes when either revision has a volume above zero and no centre, instead of "Not measured in
both", which would claim a measurement nobody attempted. Read off the two revisions the page already
holds, so the diff carries no new field. It names neither revision, because either of the two may be
the one and the pair is taken in any order; and it is worded as queued rather than as a promise of
when, because only a mesh revision is swept and a pre-bridge-8 CAD one waits on a worker that has a
CAD kernel.

**2. Range tests for the material and tag facets.** Only `format_facet`'s `field_range` was tested.
`a_number_range_narrows_the_grid_and_passes_over_words` now gives each of its three parts a material
and a tag of its own and asserts exact `(value, count)` pairs for `material_facet` and `tag_facet`,
plus the unnarrowed list, so a facet ignoring the range shows as a value **gone** rather than as a
smaller total. Deleting the `$9::jsonpath` line from either facet fails it, at the assertion for that
facet.

**3. A part's own mass on its page.** `PartDetail.mass_g`, worked out in `detail.rs` from
`PgDensities::of_part` when the page is read — one more `part` row, still the open path — as
`revisions.rs` already does per revision. Always `Approximate::tessellated`, whatever the volume's own
provenance: a density is typed and never measured, so the product is never exact. The geometry
section's ≈ key counts `massG` among its figures, because a STEP part shows an analytic volume with no
mark and would otherwise print the ≈ with nothing to explain it. Where there is no mass the page says
which of the **five** reasons it is, in the order a reader can act in: **no volume first** (an open
mesh told to set a density would still have no mass after setting one), then a part recorded as
holding none (which must not be told to give it a material — that is the decision it just made), then
no material, several materials, no density for the one it has. A blank would read as "weighs nothing".
`Densities.tsx` now invalidates `['part']` as well as `['revisions']` and `['diff']`, or the dialog would leave the mass on
the page behind it stale; its own test asserts all three.

**4. "Holds no material", said properly.** No schema change was needed and this is the finding worth
carrying: **typed-and-empty was already expressible** — `materials_typed` true with `materials = '{}'`
— and `set_metadata`'s `CASE WHEN materials_typed` already respected it. Only the API conflated the
state with the reset. So `PUT /api/parts/{id}/materials` with `[]` now says the part holds none and is
kept over what its file states, `DELETE` on the same path is the reset, and `PgParts::set_materials`
lost its CASE entirely (`materials_typed = true`, always) beside a new `PgParts::unset_materials`. The
page says "Holds no material. Nothing its file states will replace this." — **including where the list
cannot be edited**, which `WordList` used to render as nothing at all — with a "Use what the file
states" button offered exactly while there is a typed list to undo. `strings.materials.fromFile` lost
its "until you remove every material", which had become false.

The test that matters is the round trip: the file names AISI 1045 steel, `PUT []`, a second revision
of a file that names it again, and the part is still empty and still typed.
`a_typed_material_outlasts_a_revision_whose_file_states_another`'s last leg moved from
`set_materials(part, &[])` to `unset_materials(part)`, and the API test's
"an empty list hands back the file" assertions moved to `DELETE`.

**5. Material names over 64 characters — decided: saved, to 200.** Migration `0051` raises
`material_density_material_check` to `BETWEEN 1 AND 200`, and `MATERIAL_MAX` moves with it, split out
of `tags.rs`'s one `MAX_CHARS` so a **tag** stays at 64 (a tag is always typed by a person; a material
usually is not). "Stainless steel, AISI 316L, annealed, cold drawn bar to ASTM A276/A276M" is 71
characters and an ordinary thing for a supplier's STEP export to say — under the old limit that part
could never be given a density, so it could never have a mass at all. Past 200 the refusal says the
number, and the person can shorten the part's own material to match, because both routes take the same
200. 200 keeps the primary key far inside btree's ~2704-byte entry limit; `part.materials` itself stays
unbounded `text[]`, so a file naming something longer still keeps it and simply has no density.

### Decided without the owner

- **Migration `0051` went to item 5, not item 4.** Item 4 needed no schema change (above). One
  migration, one goal; no number was taken that the board did not give.
- **Item 5 saved rather than refused, at 200 characters.** The goal left the choice open. Refusing
  would have meant a whole class of ordinary STEP parts that can never carry a mass.
- **`DELETE` as the reset** rather than a flag on the `PUT` body. Tags have no counterpart, because
  nothing but a person ever gives a part one.
- **The queued centre is inferred on the page** from volume-and-no-centre rather than carried as a new
  field on `RevisionDiff`. No wire change, and the page already holds both revisions.
- **A mass is labelled approximate unconditionally**, never with the volume's provenance. `CLAUDE.md`
  says analytic values come from B-rep entities; a typed density is not one.

### A stated test that would have passed on broken code

`a_revision_with_no_centre_of_mass_is_queued_for_one_when_a_worker_starts`'s two "nothing is queued"
legs both passed for the wrong reason: `enqueue_if_absent(_, _, true)` declines while the job the test
had already dequeued is still `running`, so neither leg ever reached the sweep's predicate. Breaking
`volume > 0` into `volume IS NOT NULL` left the test green, which is how it was found. Both legs now
`DELETE FROM job` first, and the mutation fails them. Committed as its own change (`1c8e12d`) so the
reason is on the record.

Both legs were found by mutation, and so were two more: the sweep's format filter was absent until a
mutation showed a CAD revision would loop, and the mass reasons were in the wrong order until the test
gained the open-mesh and holds-none cases. **Sixteen mutations, sixteen caught**, the harness at
`target/l3-check/mutate.sh`.

### Left for later

- A bridge-8 STEP revision whose warn-only `record_centre_of_mass` failed at ingest is now reachable
  by neither sweep: its `structure` rung is current, so the stale sweep passes it, and it is not a
  mesh, so this one skips it. Rare (a database write that failed while the revision committed) and visible
  in the worker log. The row that would settle it is the same one `PgShapes::stale_revisions`' ceiling
  wants: "this kernel version found no centre for this revision".
- A mass is offered only for a part of exactly one material. A part of several has no one density, and
  summing per-body volumes would need the assembly tree, not a part row.
- The centreless sweep re-derives the whole L0 rung to get one figure. Cheap — it is a mesh, by
  construction, and the rung's bytes are content-addressed so the row lands on the same hash. A kernel
  call that measures without producing anything would be tidier, and nothing today asks for one.
- `part.materials` is unbounded while a density's key stops at 200, so a file naming a longer material
  keeps it and can never have a mass. Recorded rather than fixed: capping at ingest would rename
  somebody's material, and dropping it would put the facet and `metadata_json` out of step.
