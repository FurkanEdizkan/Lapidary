## Why

Near-duplicates and "more like this" need a shape profile per part. It is computed in the worker from the stored L0
tessellation — never on the open path, never from the source file, never by the kernel. Design:
[`phase-6.md`](phase-6.md) § Shape profile.

## Rules

- Owns: `lapidary-cad/src/{shape,glb,lib}.rs`; `JobPayload::ProfileShape { revision }` (kind `profile_shape`) and
  `Outcome::Profiled` in `lapidary-core/src/job.rs`; the `Profiled` arm in `lapidary-db/src/jobs.rs` `complete`; reads
  in `lapidary-db/src/shapes.rs` (`stale_revisions`, the L0-hash lookup); `lapidary-ingest/src/shape.rs` (new); the
  dispatch arm and two warn-only call sites in `handler.rs`; the re-profile after an L0 rebuild and the call from
  `enqueue_stale_derivatives` in `derive.rs`. **Not** `bin/lapidary-server/src/main.rs`.
- No new crates. Pure, deterministic: the same L0 twice gives bit-identical output.
- The STL corpus (`/mnt/Storage2/All/STL Files`) is read-only; use symlinks.

## Stages

1. **The profile** (`lapidary-cad/src/shape.rs`) — principal axes, D2 (65,536 pairs, splitmix64 fixed seed, 32 bins
   over `[0, 3m)`, square roots), three ratios. **Tests:** translate, rotate, permute triangles, re-tessellate a
   cylinder, scale (descriptor same, `size_mm` scales); twice gives identical bits; cube, cylinder and bracket are
   farther apart than the threshold.
2. **The job** — `ProfileShape`, the handler (decode L0, profile, `PgShapes::record`), warn-only profiling after an
   ingest and a derive commit, a re-profile when L0 is rebuilt, and `enqueue_stale_shapes` for parts whose latest
   revision has an L0 but no current profile (none, older version, other L0 hash, older revision). **Tests**
   (`crates/lapidary-ingest/tests/shape.rs`): ingesting `fixtures/bracket-lp-1042-03.stl` writes a row; a rotated copy
   at another path is a near-duplicate; deleting the row and restarting the worker writes an identical row; a
   controlled revision moves the row; a revision with no L0 fails permanently, saying why.
3. **Calibration** — nearest-neighbour distances over about 1,000 corpus parts; generated duplicates (rotated,
   translated copies); a STEP-against-STL pair from `fixtures/step`; the threshold chosen by the gap and set in
   `lapidary-core/src/shape.rs` (the one edit there G2 may make). Time to profile one L0: target under 5 ms. If there is
   no clean gap, say so: the exit falls back to identical files plus a tight threshold.
4. Mutation checks: invariance broken on purpose (e.g. skip centring), the version not bumped, a stale row not
   re-queued.

## Record

### What landed

- **`lapidary_cad::profile(positions, indices) -> Result<ShapeProfile, CadError>`**
  (`crates/lapidary-cad/src/shape.rs`), the 35 floats of `phase-6.md` exactly: 32 D2 bins from 65,536 point pairs
  (triangle by cumulative-area search, point uniform inside it, splitmix64 written out in-crate with a fixed seed,
  bins over `[0, 3m)`, overflow into the last, stored as **square roots** of the probabilities), then λ2/λ1, λ3/λ1
  and ln(area / m²). The covariance is area-weighted and exact per triangle — a triangle's own second moment about
  its centroid is `(1/12)·Σ(vᵢ − g)(vᵢ − g)ᵀ`, and its centroid's offset from the part's centre carries the rest —
  with eigenvalues from a closed-form 3×3 solver. `size_mm` is the mean surface-point distance, so an open mesh has
  one. All of it in f64 internally: a covariance sums squares of coordinates, and f32 loses the small eigenvalue on a
  part 300 mm from the origin.
- **`lapidary_cad::read_triangles`** — the GLB tests' own independent decoder promoted to production, with a shared
  `parse_glb` so there is one reader rather than two. The tests read through it, and still share no helper with the
  writer.
- **A degenerate mesh is refused, never recorded.** No triangles, zero area, every point in one place, a non-finite
  coordinate, an index past the positions: all `CadError::Unrenderable` with a message saying which. `part_shape`
  requires `size_mm > 0`, so a profile of a shapeless mesh would fail at the insert and be retried for ever.
- **The job:** `JobPayload::ProfileShape { revision }` (`profile_shape`), `Outcome::Profiled`, the arm in
  `PgJobs::complete`, the dispatch arm, and `crates/lapidary-ingest/src/shape.rs` —
  `profile_shape` (the work), `record_shape` (the same call, warn-only) and `enqueue_stale_shapes` (the start-up
  sweep, at most 5,000 a start).
- **Ingest profiles in line, and queues nothing.** `record_shape` runs inside the ingest job on both commit paths
  (new part and new revision), after `record_topology`, **before** the job returns its outcome — so a batch cannot
  reach `finishedAt` with its parts unprofiled, and no `profile_shape` row is written for an ordinary ingest at all
  (a test asserts the count is 0). A rebuilt L0 re-profiles the same way inside the `derive` job. The job kind is for
  backfill only.
- **Db reads:** `PgShapes::l0_of_revision(library, revision)` — the part and its current L0 hash, scoped through
  `part.library_id` and skipping deleted parts, so a revision id held from anywhere reaches nothing — and
  `PgShapes::stale_revisions(limit)`, which finds the four ways a row is not the profile of this part's current L0:
  no row, `version <> SHAPE_VERSION`, `l0_blake3 <>` the current rung's, `revision_id <>` the latest revision's.
  **G3 need not check `l0_blake3`:** a rebuilt L0 is caught twice on this side, in line by `derive_one` and by this
  sweep.
- Nothing in `lapidary-core/src/shape.rs` changed. `NEAR_DUPLICATE_DISTANCE` stays **0.04**.

### The sampler's noise floor: 0.011, and it is arithmetic

A bin holding probability `p` is estimated with standard deviation `√(p(1−p)/N)`; storing its square root divides that
by `2√p`, leaving `√((1−p)/(4N))` — very nearly `1/(2√N)` whatever `p` is. Over 32 bins that is
`√(BINS / (4·PAIRS))` = **0.011** between two samplings of one shape. Measured 0.013 for a cylinder whose triangles
were reordered. **No threshold can mean anything below that**, which is why the invariance tests' tolerance is 0.02
and not 0.005, and why `PAIRS` was left at the design's 65,536: halving the floor costs four times the pairs, and one
profile is already 4.3 ms.

### How the threshold was calibrated

`crates/lapidary-cad/tests/calibration.rs`, `#[ignore]`d, built under the compile lock and run in release outside it;
report in `target/calibration-G2.txt`. 974 of the 1,000 indexed corpus STLs (26 unreadable or unparsed, none over the
64 MB cap), each through the production path — parse, cluster to L0, write the GLB, decode it, profile. The corpus was
read only, through the `size<TAB>path` index copied into this lane's own `target/`.

**Nearest neighbour inside the ±2% size band** (the only comparison the rule ever makes; 966 parts have one,
31,630 ordered pairs are in band):

| percentile | 1% | 5% | 10% | 25% | 50% | 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| distance | 3.7e-9 | 0.0188 | 0.0289 | 0.0496 | 0.0942 | 0.4235 |

Parts with an in-band neighbour under `d`: 0.005 → 16 · 0.01 → 16 · 0.02 → 54 · 0.03 → 103 · **0.04 → 170** ·
0.06 → 316 · 0.12 → 552.

**There is no gap above the noise floor.** 12 parts sit at exactly 0 (6 pairs, 5 of them byte-identical by hash — the
"Identical" rule's, not the threshold's) and 4 more just above it; nothing else is closer than 0.0112, which is the
floor itself rather than structure in the corpus. Above it the distribution is continuous: the closest non-identical
pairs are left/right mirror halves of one model (`…_L.stl` / `…_R.stl`, 0.0113–0.0169) and same-family bases, and it
runs smoothly upward with no second gap. So the exit G2.md wrote for this case applies — **the tight starting
threshold is kept rather than a number invented.** At 0.04, 170 of 966 parts (17.6%) get at least one proposal, high
because this slice of the corpus is largely miniature bases, and every one of them is something a person would
reasonably be shown.

### Generated duplicates: the descriptor is invariant, the L0 rung is not a stable input

63 corpus parts, each copy through the same L0 path a re-ingest takes, both sides of every comparison at L0 except the
last row:

| copy | median | p90 | median without ln(A/m²) | size_mm moved (median / p90) | out of the ±2% band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| moved 250 mm | 0.0002 | 0.0005 | 0.0002 | 0.0000 / 0.0000 | 0 of 63 |
| scaled 1% | 0.0001 | 0.0002 | 0.0001 | 0.0100 / 0.0100 | 0 of 63 |
| **rotated 90° about z** | **0.0000** | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 / 0.0000 | 0 of 63 |
| same surface, subdivided 4× | **0.0509** | 0.2637 | 0.0284 | 0.0089 / 0.0428 | 15 of 63 |
| rotated 37° about (1,2,3) | **0.0907** | 0.2692 | 0.0354 | 0.0084 / 0.0301 | 16 of 63 |
| L0 against the L1 rung | 0.1250 | 0.2977 | 0.0416 | 0.0125 / 0.0370 | 22 of 63 |

Read the third and fourth rows together and the cause is unambiguous. **A quarter turn, which leaves the bounding box
axis-aligned, moves the descriptor by nothing at all; an off-axis turn moves it by 0.09; and the same surface
triangulated four times as finely — same bounding box, same grid — moves it by 0.05.** So it is not rotation and not
re-tessellation that the descriptor fails on. It is that **clustering to L0 snaps vertices to a 32-cell grid over the
part's bounding box, so which vertices share a cell depends on the triangulation and on how the box is oriented**, and
two L0 rungs of one surface differ by several per cent of area. `ln(area / m²)` carries that straight through: mean
block contributions for a rotated copy are D2 0.0298, λ2/λ1 0.0299, λ3/λ1 0.0098, **ln(A/m²) 0.1024** of a 0.111
total. And no threshold repairs it, because a quarter of those copies leave the ±2% size band and are never
candidates.

This contradicts `phase-6.md`'s "L0's clustering smooths the difference between an STL and a STEP of the same part":
for the area term it does the opposite. **Left for the lead and the owner**, with what each option costs:

1. **Drop or replace `ln(area / m²)`** (34 floats). Rotated-37° median falls to 0.0354 and subdivided to 0.0284, both
   inside 0.04 — but corpus parts with an in-band neighbour under 0.04 rise from 170 to 368, so the review queue
   roughly doubles. It is also a `SHAPE_VERSION` bump and a contract G3 builds against.
2. **Profile a finer rung** (L1, or L2) where the decimation is gentler. Costs a rung that ingest does not build, so
   the profile stops being free at ingest.
3. **Accept it**: rotated re-exports are found by eye, and the shape profile serves identical, mirrored and
   genuinely-similar parts, which it does well.

Simple parts are unaffected and are tested: the bracket fixture turned 37°, written back out as STL and ingested at
another path, **is** a near-duplicate, and so is a cylinder through its L0 rung. It is the dense organic meshes that
move.

### Time to profile one L0

Release, machine idle (load 0.76), 974 parts: **median 4.33 ms, p95 4.77 ms, worst 6.82 ms**, of which the GLB decode
is 0.03 ms at the median. L0 triangle counts: median 2,822, worst 5,668. Under the goal's 5 ms target at the median
and the 95th percentile, over it in the worst case. Almost all of it is the 65,536-pair sampler; the covariance and
the eigenvalues are linear in the triangle count and negligible.

### Mirror images

Identical by construction — every ingredient is a distance, an area or an eigenvalue, and reflection changes none of
them. Documented in `shape.rs`'s header, tested (`a_mirror_image_looks_identical_and_is_meant_to`), and visible in the
corpus: the closest non-identical pairs are `_L`/`_R` halves of one model. `part_link`'s `distinct` is the answer, as
the design says.

### Mutations caught

`target/mutations/mutate-g2.sh`, 10 of 10, each restored and checked by sha256:

| mutation | caught by |
|---|---|
| covariance about the origin, not the centroid | `moving_a_part_does_not_change_its_shape` |
| bins in millimetres, not multiples of the mean | `scaling_a_part_keeps_its_descriptor_and_scales_its_size` |
| D2 stored as probabilities, not square roots | `the_d2_block_holds_the_square_roots_of_a_whole_distribution` |
| `version <> SHAPE_VERSION` dropped from the sweep | `a_row_of_another_version_or_another_rung_is_swept_up` |
| `l0_blake3 <>` dropped from the sweep | the same |
| `revision_id <>` dropped from the sweep | `a_new_revision_moves_the_row_to_its_own_rung` |
| `part.library_id` dropped from `l0_of_revision` | `a_revision_with_no_viewer_mesh_fails_once_and_says_what_to_do` |
| ingest stops profiling what it wrote | three tests |
| a rebuilt L0 stops re-profiling | `rebuilding_the_viewer_mesh_profiles_the_part_again` |
| the start-up sweep stops running | three tests |

The first run had two survivors and both were real gaps, not weak mutations: nothing in `lapidary-cad` checked that
the D2 block holds square roots (the ingest test did, in the wrong crate), and the cross-library case was asked
*after* the rung had been deleted, so a missing rung refused the job for the wrong reason and the library check proved
nothing. Both tests were fixed and all ten are caught.

### Decided without the owner

- **Kept `NEAR_DUPLICATE_DISTANCE` at 0.04** and changed nothing in `lapidary-core/src/shape.rs`, on the fallback
  this goal file already agreed.
- **Kept `PAIRS` at 65,536** and recorded the noise floor instead of raising it: four times the pairs to halve the
  floor would put one profile well past 5 ms.
- `read_triangles` **replaced** the GLB tests' private decoder rather than standing beside it, with `parse_glb`
  shared; the "shares no helper with the writer" property still holds and there is one decoder to be wrong.
- The sweep queues through `enqueue_if_absent` **per part**, capped at 5,000 a worker start, rather than one
  `INSERT … SELECT`: a batch statement belongs in `jobs.rs`, which this goal does not own.
- A corrupt or shapeless stored rung reuses **`CadError::Unrenderable`** rather than adding a variant to
  `kernel.rs`, which this goal does not own. The actionable wording is on the job's message, which is what a person
  sees.
- `enqueue_stale_shapes` runs from `enqueue_stale_derivatives` **beside** the rung sweep, not inside it: a part can
  be missing a profile while every rung it has is current, and every part ingested before Phase 6 is.
